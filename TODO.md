- [ ] **A live tracking link while recording** (asked for 2026-11-01). An option, when you start recording any sport, to share a link that friends and family open in any browser, with no account, to see where you are, the track so far, and distance, time and speed or pace, as Garmin LiveTrack or Strava Beacon do. On the iPhone, Apple Watch and Android. **The server side is built** (`services/live_share.py`, `routes/live_share.py`, `templates/live.html`, `static/js/live.js`, `tests/test_live_share.py`): `POST /api/v1/live/share {recording, sport, track}` starts a link for the recording (or gives the running one, its token kept hashed and sealed) and refuses with a 409 and `settings_url` when the outside address is missing, not https, or one that only works at home; `DELETE` stops it. `/api/v1/live` now takes `recording`, and a share takes only its own recording's readings, thinned to a point every 10 s or 25 m in `LivePoint`. `GET /live/<token>` is public, rate-limited per address, polls `/data?since=` every 10 s, and shows the first name, sport, distance, time, speed or pace now and on average, battery, "No signal since" and "Finished", but no position or track within 300 m of where the track began. A link ends an hour after an idle reading, 6 h after its last reading, or at once when stopped; an unknown token gets the same 404 page. The worker's `live.go_idle` loop runs `live_share.sweep`, which deletes an ended share's points. The iPhone app sends `recording` with every live post (the recording's id from Start, also its upload's `client_id`) and starts, sends and stops links (`ios/Headwind/Record/LiveShareController.swift`, `ios/Shared/LiveShare.swift`). The Watch posts its own readings every 30 s, starts its recording's link with its own token and hands it to the iPhone (`LiveLinkMessage`), which offers it in a notification to tap and send (`LiveLinkNotifier`). A new link is emailed by the server to the contacts chosen beforehand (`services/contacts.py`, `services/mail.py`, Settings → Contacts and Email on the web, `/api/v1/contacts` and the iPhone's Settings), once per share and by the worker; the phone and the Watch say who it went to.

## Not doing

Decided against, with the reason, so they aren't looked into again.

- **Garmin's official sign-in.** Its OAuth (the Connect Developer Program) is for businesses only, and pushes data to a public HTTPS address on each server, so it doesn't fit a self-hosted app (decided 2026-10-20). Headwind signs in through garminconnect and keeps only its tokens.
- **Rewriting the server in another language** (Go, Rust, TypeScript). The libraries it leans on are Python's: garminconnect (nothing else signs in to Garmin this way), fitparse / fitdecode, gpxpy, the Anthropic and OpenAI SDKs. Python runs on a Pi and in the Windows build, and a rewrite would end the PRs to upstream. What's slow is FIT decoding and stream maths in plain Python, which **Decide whether to swap fitparse for a faster FIT decoder** and numpy (**Training metrics**) cover (architecture review, 2026-11-01).
- **Postgres or another database server.** SQLite is one file to back up, needs no setup and runs on a Pi, and holds up for a household or a club. Its one writer at a time is handled by the background worker (`services/worker.py`), not by a server (2026-11-01).
- **An ORM (SQLAlchemy).** Tens of thousands of lines of SQL to move for little gain. What multi-user needs is one helper for whose data a request is about (**Proper multi-user support, with roles**), and migrations are numbered steps (`database.STEPS`) (2026-11-01).
- **An async framework (FastAPI, Quart).** The slow parts are CPU (decoding files), not waiting on the network, so async wouldn't make them faster (2026-11-01).
- **A front-end framework or build step** (React and the like). Server-rendered pages suit a self-hosted app and the Windows build; page scripts move to `static/js` as ES modules (**Web assets: serve the libraries locally, cache and compress**) (2026-11-01).
- **Replacing the Swift apps with a cross-platform one** (Flutter, React Native). HealthKit, the Bluetooth sensors and the Watch need native code (2026-11-01).
- **OS Leisure maps.** The OS Maps API publishes it only in British National Grid (EPSG:27700), and its useful zooms (6-9) are Premium. Every map here is Web Mercator; Leisure would need Proj4Leaflet and a map in 27700 of its own. OS Outdoor in 3857 is used instead, on the OpenData zooms only.
